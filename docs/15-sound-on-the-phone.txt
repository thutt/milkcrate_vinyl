SOUND ON THE PHONE
==================

Volume leveling and the volume are under "..." > Volume.  The equalizer
is the EQ button in the top bar.  They apply to the phone only.

Volume and leveling
-------------------

Each song's loudness was measured once and is stored in the database
(see Creating the database).  Loudness is in LUFS: a negative number,
and the closer to zero the louder.  10 LUFS is about twice the loudness
of 20 LUFS.

  Volume leveling   On: songs louder than the target are turned down to
                    it.  Off: every song plays at its own level.
  Target            -30 to -8 LUFS.  Default -10.
  Volume            0 to 1.  Default 1.

The app cannot make a quiet song louder, only turn a loud one down.  A
song quieter than the target plays at its own level.  The phone's own
volume, and that of Bluetooth headphones or a car, then sets how loud
it is.

The defaults apply only until a slider has been moved.  The app then
keeps the value, also across updates.  A target of -8 turns leveling
almost off: most songs are quieter than that.

Equalizer
---------

The EQ button in the top bar opens the equalizer with one tap, from any
tab.  It is in the accent colour while the equalizer is on.  "..." >
Equalizer... opens it too.  It is off by default; off, the sound is not
processed at all.

A line above the seek bar always says what the equalizer is doing:
"Equalizer off", "Equalizer: Rock" or "Equalizer: Classical (auto)".
It is text only; the EQ button changes the settings.

  Equalizer on      Switches it on or off.  Switched on, it takes
                    effect with the next song.  Switched off, the
                    song playing goes on with flat settings, and the
                    next song plays without the equalizer.
  Preset            A list of settings, below.  The box reads "Custom"
                    when the sliders match none.
  Sliders           Five bands, -12 to +12 dB each: 60 Hz, 230 Hz,
                    910 Hz, 3.6 kHz and 14 kHz.

Moving a slider or choosing a preset changes the sound at once while
the equalizer is on.  The settings are kept between runs.  A boost does
not lower the overall level; peaks that a boost would push past full
scale are rounded off.

Presets (dB for the five bands, low to high):

  Flat                  0   0   0   0   0
  Normal               +3   0   0   0  +3
  Rock                 +5  +3  -1  +3  +5
  Metal                +4  +1  +9  +3   0
  Punk                 +3   0  -2  +4  +3
  Pop                  -1  +2  +5  +1  -2
  Hip-hop / R&B        +5  +3   0  +1  +3
  Electronic / Dance   +6   0  +2  +4  +1
  Jazz                 +4  +2  -2  +2  +5
  Classical            +5  +3  -2  +4  +4
  Folk / Acoustic      +3   0   0  +2  -1
  Country              +2  +1  +1  +3  +2
  Vocal                -2  -1  +3  +4  +1
  Bass boost           +7  +5  +1   0   0
  Treble boost          0   0   0  +4  +7
  Loudness             +6  +3   0  +2  +5

Normal, Rock, Metal, Pop, Hip-hop / R&B, Electronic / Dance, Jazz,
Classical and Folk / Acoustic are the presets of Android's own
equalizer (called Normal, Rock, Heavy Metal, Pop, Hip Hop, Dance, Jazz,
Classical and Folk there), which has the same five bands.  The others
are our own guesses.  The values are starting points, not a standard.

In the equalizer dialog the preset's name is shown between a left and a
right arrow.  The arrows select the previous or the next preset in the
order above, round the list, and the sound changes at once.  After the
sliders were moved by hand the name is "Custom"; the left arrow then
selects the last preset and the right arrow the first.

The bars
--------

The three bars beside the playing song (in the song list and on the
playlist row) move in time with the song: each bar picks a new random
height every beat, every half beat or every three quarters of a beat,
using the song's BPM tag.  A song with a BPM of 0, or none, is taken as 100;
values are kept between 40 and 240.  The top of a tall bar is red.
Paused, the bars sink slowly to their lowest height.

Saved presets
-------------

"Save as..." under the sliders keeps the five sliders under a name.
Give a name that is not a built-in preset's (the name "Custom" is also
taken); a name already saved is replaced.  A saved preset joins the
arrow list after the built-in ones, in alphabetical order, and is
shown by name whenever the sliders match it.  "Save as..." is locked
while Automatic shows a song's preset.  "Delete" removes the saved
preset whose name is shown; the sliders stay where they are and the
name becomes "Custom".  Built-in presets cannot be deleted.

With Automatic on, a saved preset is tried before the built-in rules:
it is used for a song when one of the song's genres contains the
preset's name, capitals ignored.  Name a preset "Prog" and a song with
the genre "Progressive Rock; Prog Metal" gets it, not Rock or Metal.
When several saved presets match, the one with the longest name wins.
Saved presets are kept between runs, on the phone only.

Manual and automatic settings
-----------------------------

The equalizer works in one of two ways, chosen by the switch
"Auto: a preset for each song's genre":

                 Manual (switch off)     Automatic (switch on)
  Who chooses    You: a preset or the    The app, for each song, from
                 sliders.                the song's genre tags.
  Applies to     Every song.             One song at a time; the next
                 It stays until you      song may get another preset.
                 change it.
  Preset name    Left and right arrows   Shows the song's preset;
  and sliders    step through the        arrows and sliders locked.
                 presets; the sliders
                 are free.
  Saved          Yes, between runs.      The switch is saved; the
                                         songs' presets are not.
  Song with no   Not applicable.         The manual settings are used.
  matching genre

The manual settings are one set for the whole app.  Automatic mode
never changes them.  It sets them aside while a song has a preset and
uses them again for a song that has none.  Switching Automatic off
returns to them, with the arrows and the sliders unlocked.

In automatic mode the preset is chosen when a song starts and is kept
until it ends.  Changing the switch during a song takes effect at once
for that song.  Moving the sliders is not possible while a song has a
preset, so to adjust the sound, switch Automatic off, or play a song
that has no preset.

The line above the seek bar tells the two apart: "Equalizer: Rock" is
the manual setting and "Equalizer: Rock (auto)" is a preset chosen for
the song.  Both only apply while the equalizer is on.

Automatic equalizer
-------------------

"Auto: a preset for each song's genre" chooses the preset from the
song's genre tags each time a song starts.  It works only while the
equalizer is on.  A line under the switch names the preset in use for
the song playing, and the preset name and the sliders show that preset.
They are locked while it applies, because changing them would change
settings that are not used.  A song with no matching genre uses the
manual settings, and the arrows and sliders unlock.

Saved presets (above) are tried first.  Otherwise:

A genre selects a preset when its name contains one of these words
(capitals do not matter).  The presets are tried in this order, and the
first that fits any of the song's genres is used:

  Metal                metal
  Punk                 punk
  Hip-hop / R&B        hip hop, hip-hop, rap, r&b, rnb, soul, funk
  Electronic / Dance   electronic, dance, techno, house, trance, edm,
                       synth
  Rock                 rock
  Jazz                 jazz, big band, swing, bebop, blues
  Classical            classical, orchestral, symphon, opera
  Country              country, bluegrass
  Folk / Acoustic      acoustic, folk, celtic, hawaiian, singer
  Pop                  pop, new wave, 80s, disco
  Vocal                audiobook, spoken, podcast, speech

A song with several genres gets the first preset in the list that any
of them selects: "Rock; Metal" gets Metal, and "Workout; Rock" gets
Rock.  "Symphonic Rock" gets Rock, not Classical, because Rock comes
first.

A song whose genres match none, for example "Workout", "Roadtrip" or
"Soundtrack", or a song with no genre, gets the preset or sliders set
by hand.  Genres are read from the database on the phone, so a
change made in the database editor reaches the phone with the next
sync.

Output changes
--------------

The music pauses when its output goes away: Bluetooth headphones turned
off or out of range, a car unit that switches off or disconnects, wired
or USB headphones unplugged.  It never carries on from the phone's
speaker.  Connecting an output does not start or stop anything.  Press
Play to continue.  The pause is logged under the tag MilkcrateVinyl.

The pause works while the app has a song loaded, because it is done by
the playback service.

Files that do not start
-----------------------

Some MP3 files stay at 0:00 and make no sound although the player says
it plays; moving the position slider starts them.  The cause is in Qt's
media code: files that have both a cover picture and a LAME or Xing
header, which most MP3 encoders write, are affected, and other files may
be.  When a song is still at 0:00 after 1.5 seconds the app moves to the
start itself, which makes it play, and tries up to three times.  The song
then starts about 1.5 seconds late.  The desktop player does the same.
The file is not changed.

    scripts/find-stalled-songs [--jobs N]

lists the songs that are affected: it probes every track of the database
with ffprobe (part of ffmpeg) and prints the paths, relative to the music
root, of the files that have a cover picture and an audio stream that
starts after time 0.  Standard output holds only the paths; a summary
goes to standard error.  Nothing is changed.  A library of 13,000 files
takes about a minute.  In one library, 1,289 of 13,383 songs (all MP3)
matched, and every one of 20 matches tried did stall, and none of 40
others.

The log file
------------

The app writes what it logs to milkcrate-log.txt in the library folder,
next to tags.db, with a time on each line.  Unlike the system log
(adb logcat) it survives switching the phone off.  It starts over at 4
MB; the old file is kept as milkcrate-log.txt.1.  Lines to look for
when the sound stutters:

    engine thread waited N ms for the database (...)
    engine thread did not feed the sound for N ms
    sound output ran dry at N ms
    sound buffer came N ms after the one before
    song change took N ms (...)
    opening the sound output took N ms
    database write (play count | played | rating) took N ms

Each song change logs how long every step took, so a stall can be
placed.  Buffers of the song before, still on their way when the song
is changed, are dropped, and the log says how many.  Pause, resume,
seek and a song chosen from a list are logged with the position.

Copy the file to the computer with adb pull or a file manager.

How the sound is played
-----------------------

With the equalizer on, the app decodes the song and plays it through a
sound output of its own.  Every change of song, and every seek, closes
that output and opens a new one.  Keeping one output and stopping and
starting it again for each song caused a steady stutter over Bluetooth
after a few quick changes of song; it lasted until the song was
restarted.  The play counts, ratings and "played" marks are written to
the database in a thread of their own, so a slow card does not
interrupt the sound.

Limits
------

  * With the equalizer on, the app does its own output instead of the
    player's: the sound is 16-bit stereo.  A song with more than two
    channels is mixed down to the first two.
  * Pausing can leave about 150 ms of sound to play out.
  * When the output device does not take a song's sample rate, the
    sound is converted to the device's rate, with simple interpolation.
  * The equalizer is not in the desktop programs.
